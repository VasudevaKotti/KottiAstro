## Dasha flow: finding the delivering planet

- A dasha lord's delivering planet is found with five rules checked strictly in order. Stop at the first rule that applies and never jump ahead; for example, when an exchange exists, Rule 3 is never reached. `[0:10:18]`
- Rule 1 (check first): a planet in its own star, or in a star exchange (each planet in the other's nakshatra), follows a sign lord instead of its star lord. In an exchange the planet is treated as sitting in its partner's sign and follows that sign's lord (the "effective star lord"), giving results from where that lord sits. Example: Venus in the Moon's star with the Moon in Leo acts from Leo and follows the Sun. `[0:06:12]`
- Nodes follow the same rule: with Rahu in Jupiter's star and Jupiter in Rahu's star, Rahu is placed in Jupiter's sign and follows that sign's lord (Jupiter in Libra, so Venus). It does not follow the lord of the sign Rahu occupies, and not Jupiter. `[0:55:07]`
- In an exchange, the condition of the effective star lord drives the whole dasha (a gandanta pada there is a major problem). Side factors count only a little. `[0:29:35]`
- Rule 2: a planet in its own sign or exalted follows its original star lord directly, giving results from where that star lord sits. Rule 2 comes before Rules 3 and 4, so any planets occupying its stars are ignored. `[0:09:16]`
- Rule 2 is skipped for Rahu and Ketu, which own no sign. `[0:58:12]`
- Rule 3: when two or more planets occupy the dasha lord's nakshatras, follow the dasha lord's own star lord. `[0:10:18]`
- Rule 3 still applies when one of several occupants is debilitated. Dropping the debilitated one so that Rule 4 applies has not been tested. `[0:47:55]`
- Default: when no rule applies, follow the star lord. Only Rule 1 (the effective star lord) and Rule 4 depart from the star lord. About 80–90% of cases simply follow it, but missing one of the rare Rule 4 cases makes predictions fail without an obvious reason. `[0:11:18]`
- Outside Rule 4, the dasha lord and its star lord act together, weighted 25% to the planet and 75% to the star lord. The dasha lord's own lordships and placement show in small measure; the star lord gives most of the results. `[0:09:16]`
- Resolve one level only. Once the delivering planet is found, judge it, but don't apply the five rules to it again: no own-star jump to its sign lord, no check of who occupies its stars, no move on to its own star lord. Those checks belong to that planet's own dasha. `[0:41:48]`
- A dasha activates the houses the dasha lord owns and the house of its effective star lord. With a star exchange, the houses of both exchanging planets activate as well, so good and bad events run in parallel (e.g. a sudden job loss followed by an unexpected new job). `[0:31:37]`

## Rule 4 (R4): single occupant

- If exactly one planet occupies the dasha lord's nakshatras, the dasha acts as that occupant's dasha. The occupant delivers alone, mainly from its own house, with no joint action with the dasha lord. `[0:10:18]`
- The outcome then depends on the occupant: good if it is sound, poor if it is retrograde or afflicted. `[0:14:21]`
- Exception: a debilitated single occupant is ignored; follow the dasha lord's star lord instead. `[0:11:18]`
- R4 is a special case: the dasha lord's own retrogression or missing Light does not block the results, because the occupant delivers directly. No other rule has this property. `[0:47:55]`
- Under R4 the dasha lord's own factors still make up about 25% of the result. Its own house shows only slightly. A gandanta pada, a star lord ruling the 3rd or 6th, or a badhaka link can make the process laborious (restless hard work) even when the outcome is good. `[0:23:29]`
- Under R4, the dasha of a debilitated planet can be excellent when its only star-occupant is strong. The occupant's own dasha, which follows the debilitated planet as its star lord, stays average. `[0:49:57]`

## Judging the delivering planet

- Planet capacity comes from Light, Performance and Resource. Any dasha or bhukti needs at least Light and Performance. Resource can run a little high or low, but without Light and Performance the matter doesn't happen. `[0:45:54]`
- Light here means that the planet's star lord is not in a dusthana. `[0:45:54]`
- The dasha lord, its star lord and the bhukti lord each need proper Light, or at least a Light exception. One planet's Light doesn't make up for another's. `[0:45:54]`
- Check the delivering planet first for retrogression and for a link to Rahu or Ketu, then for Light. If it is retrograde or joined by a node, the matter fails. `[0:40:47]`
- A debilitated star lord brings its debilitation into the dasha, even when it is a yogakaraka, so results need hard work. `[0:40:47]`
- A planet that sits in the 11th bhava itself is mostly safe, as if exalted or in its own sign. This placement covers a missing Light and gives a minimum guarantee. With a weak star lord, such a dasha rates about average (~65%): results come through hard work. `[0:40:47]`
- A star lord in the 8th still lets the dasha work, but through twists and narrow escapes. `[0:45:54]`
- When the delivering planet shares a sign with other planets, the strongest of them (the "head of the house") overrides it. A good overriding planet improves the result; a bad one makes it hard. `[0:26:31]`
- Strength here is judged mainly by dig bala. A planet can also gain dig bala through a link to its dig-bala house: by sitting in the star of that house's lord (Mercury in the lagna lord's star), or by being aspected by that lord, either directly or through its star lord. The planet aspecting the lord does not count `(?)`. `[0:25:31]`
- If none of the planets in the sign has dig bala, the one earlier in the planetary sequence leads (Saturn over Mars, Sun over Mercury) `(?)`. `[1:15:32]`
- Own-sign placement protects: it overrides a conjunction dosha such as Rahu's, and it partly shields a planet whose star lord is poor. `[0:17:24]`
- A planet in its own star holds firm despite a Ketu link (Ketu in the same `(?)` Venus-ruled nakshatra). There are ups and downs, but the overall graph rises. `[0:56:09]`
- A star exchange gives protective strength. A negative outcome reached through the effective star lord is softened: score about −70 instead of −100. `[0:29:35]`
- Own sign, exchange, and placement in the lagna or the 11th create exceptions that soften the default reading, as retrogression does. Such lives run in "good screenplay": the main event succeeds, with only minor irritations. `[0:29:35]`
- Enmity between the dasha lord and its star lord (e.g. Sun and Saturn) may spoil a bhukti or two, not the whole dasha. A star lord in pushkara is very good. `[0:44:51]`

## Retrogression and nodes

- A retrograde dasha lord loses the matters of its own houses (e.g. as 3rd/4th lord: the mother's health). A yoga delivered through a strong star lord still comes (profession, finances, growth): "the bhava goes, the yoga is given". `[0:44:51]`
- When the effective star lord is the Sun, which never turns retrograde, the dasha has no retrogression effect. `[0:08:16]`
- A star lord conjunct Rahu in the same nakshatra gives the dasha a retrogression (vakra) effect. In a different nakshatra the conjunction does no harm. How to handle the same-nakshatra case was left open. `[0:19:24]`
- In a star exchange with Rahu, the retrogression of the partner planet (Jupiter) is mostly harmless `(?)`. `[0:57:11]`

## Dual lordship and dasha halves

- A planet that owns two houses serves both only while neither holds another planet. If another planet occupies one of them, that occupant runs it and the lord acts for its other house (e.g. Mars for Scorpio lagna acts as 6th lord when a planet sits in the lagna). `[0:50:59]`
- A 12th lord in the 12th gives only an average dasha (~65–70%). If it also owns an empty 9th, it acts as 9th lord too and does well; placed in the 9th, it would deliver fully. `[0:17:24]`
- Split each dasha into two halves. When the dasha planet or its star lord owns two signs, each half carries one of the lordships (e.g. results delivered by a 9th/12th lord: first half good, second half neutral, with a small disturbance in a Rahu bhukti during the second half). `[1:40:02]`
- The lordship of the moolatrikona sign usually comes first (for Mars, Aries before Scorpio). Retrogression reverses the order, and occupants can change it, so check each chart instead of fixing a rule. `[1:42:02]`
- When neither owned house is occupied, weigh both lordships, of the planet and of its star lord. Usually one half of the dasha is lost, and dashas that deliver all the way through are rare (results delivered by a 6th/11th lord: first ten years good, next ten average with health trouble). `[1:41:02]`
- A 12th lord linked to the 8th (dusthana to dusthana) is not a fault, but it works only about halfway `(?)`. `[0:30:35]`

## House analysis

- A house is judged from its occupant, its lord and its karaka. For now, work by house (sign); the bhava refinement comes later with the calculations. `[1:08:24]`
- Don't assume the lord gives a house's results; apply the dasha-flow rules to the house. `[1:09:24]`
- Empty house: look at the lord's nakshatras. A single planet there delivers all of the house's results; with several planets there, or none, the lord's star lord delivers them. `[1:09:24]`
- Occupied house: go from the occupant straight to its star lord, after applying the exchange/own-star rule (an occupant in a star exchange moves to its partner's sign and follows that sign's lord). Whether a single planet in the occupant's stars would take over, as in R4, was not addressed `(?)`. `[1:11:26]`
- An occupant's bhava position doesn't matter here; it counts for the house it sits in. `[1:26:46]`
- With two or more occupants, the strongest one (the head of the house) rules the house. `[1:15:32]`
- Aspects are used almost only (90–95%) to establish connections. `[1:12:28]`
- Judge houses separately from dashas. A good dasha of a planet doesn't make its house good by default. `[1:15:32]`
- If the ruling occupant is sound, a weak co-occupant spoils the house only during its own dasha. `[1:16:33]`
- The occupant matters most. An excellent lord and karaka can't make the house easy if the occupant is bad, although the karaka's or the lord's dasha still brings something. When occupant, lord and karaka are all spoiled, the matter is denied (e.g. no marriage). `[1:39:01]`
- A lagna occupant that delivers the 11th makes a "labha-yoga" native, who progresses and builds assets even with the lagna lord in the 12th. `[1:13:30]`

## House scoring

- Score each house out of 100: occupant 60, lord 20, karaka 20. `[1:18:38]`
- Use the dasha flow only for the occupant's 60. Score the lord and the karaka from their own star lords directly. `[1:18:38]`
- The occupant's 60 decides the house. When it is sound, the native pulls through with small problems even if the other 40 are weak; when it fails, the house fails. `[1:19:38]`
- Score the delivering planet by its capacity (Light, Performance, Resource) and adjust for retrogression. `[1:21:41]`
- A retrograde ruling occupant whose star lord is in a dusthana spoils the house. Even after it turns direct, the house keeps giving dusthana results. `[1:16:33]`
- Such an occupant scores 25 of 60 while the retrogression lasts, which counts as a fail. `[1:18:38]`
- An occupant in a star exchange scores about 45 of 60 (40–45). `[1:20:39]`
- A dusthana link is mild when the house lord owns that dusthana too (a 5th lord that also rules the 12th, or a 9th lord that also rules the 8th): about 45 of 60, less if retrograde. `[1:20:39]`
- An unafflicted 8th-house occupant that delivers the 12th is a kind of viparita raja yoga with no major fault: about 45 of 60. A node in the 7th following a yogakaraka in the 11th scores about 48. `[1:25:45]`
- Lord part: a lord in the 12th with its star lord in the 8th keeps about 15 of 20 (captions say 15, which is generous for a double dusthana link `(?)`). A karaka in the 8th with its star lord in the 12th scores low (no figure given). `[1:18:38]`
- Approximate scores are enough; decimals aren't needed. `[1:30:52]`

## Bhava vs house

- A planet in the 12th house but the 11th bhava (lagna at late degrees) gains from the bhava. `[0:20:26]`
- A star lord in the 1st house but the 12th bhava delivers from the 12th, carrying its debilitation if it is debilitated. `[0:40:47]`
- A star lord in a dusthana by sign but outside that bhava is mostly fine (~65–70%). `[1:07:21]`

## Dasha activation timing

- A mahadasha gives its main results only after a set share of it has run: (lagna degree + dasha lord's degree, each within its sign) / 60 × 100%. Lagna at 15° and Venus at 18° give 55%, about 11 years into a 20-year Venus dasha (captions say about 10). `[1:43:03]`
- For a bhukti, the formula is (lagna degree + mahadasha lord's degree + bhukti lord's degree) / 90 × 100% of the bhukti: 15 + 18 + 22 = 55 gives 61%. `[1:46:05]`
- The same percentage can be applied to each half of the dasha as well as to the whole dasha, giving three marks. `[1:45:05]`
- Late degrees push the results to the very end, like a mrita (dead) avastha, sometimes into later dashas. Lagna at 29° and Venus at 29° give about 95% (captions say "58", then "29 + 29 = 57 → 95%"; 58/60 is about 97%). `[1:45:05]`
- Until a dasha activates, the previous dasha's results continue; once active, it settles them and moves on. `[1:48:10]`
- Before activation the dasha is not lost: bhuktis still bring promotions or bonuses. The activation brings the decisive event, good or bad (a long-sought marriage, or a big loss late in the dasha), and with good support, results spike (10 becomes 100). `[1:50:12]`
- An event needs the dasha to be active, a favourable progression at that time (a sound mind to perform) and luck. Luck is hard to get (about 25% `(?)`), since malefics outnumber benefics. `[1:48:10]`
- A lagna at the very end of its sign (28–29°) is no fault. Activation follows the degree formula, and the delay is build-up time, not waste. Don't judge a dasha lost before its time. `[1:52:14]`

## Progression and the dynamic chart

- Star relationships change as planets move. When a planet changes nakshatra by progression, forward or retrograde, use its new position, and recheck the positions each year with the progression chart. The captions are garbled on which position to take when a planet moves back versus forward `(?)`. `[0:07:14]`
- A star exchange can dissolve later (e.g. when one planet turns retrograde), and the dasha reading changes with it. `[0:06:12]`
- A natal retrograde planet that rules a house causes trouble only until it turns direct by progression, at some age (e.g. 5–10 years); after that the house recovers. `[1:27:48]`
- A natal house promise (e.g. a planet's 11th-house results) holds for life unless that planet progresses into another house and turns retrograde `(?)`. While dasha and bhukti are good, the results keep coming. `[1:14:31]`
- A weak house (e.g. 40 marks) doesn't bring daily trouble: its problems lie dormant like time bombs while the dasha is good. The event happens when the dasha flow links to that house negatively and the progression turns negative at the same time. `[1:30:52]`

## Rahu- and Ketu-oriented charts

- A chart is Rahu-oriented when Rahu is in the 1st, the 2nd or an upachaya (3, 6, 10, 11), and Ketu-oriented when Ketu is there. Every chart is one or the other. `[1:28:49]`
- Rahu is the trend: a Rahu-oriented native follows whatever pays at the time and prospers when the outer world does. `[1:34:57]`
- A Ketu-oriented native goes against the trend and rises in crises (as doctors and pharma did during COVID). Such a native solves complex or unknown problems and may be promoted while others lose jobs, but may lose a job when the trend is good. `[1:34:57]`
- World cycles: 2008 and 2020 were destruction years, and 2033 is predicted as the next (2043 is also marked `(?)`). A Rahu-oriented chart may escape two down-cycles (1988, 2000) but gets hit in the third (2008). `[1:35:58]`
- Rarely (about 1 chart in 1000), a native gets opportunities in neither good nor bad times. The trend is the world's general condition, separate from the native's own dasha. `[1:36:58]`
- When things go well for some people, others meet the opposite in varying proportions. Event prediction applies about 25 such life patterns to every chart. `[1:38:00]`

## Yogas, doshas and significations

- Ubhaya kendradhipati dosha: a natural benefic that owns two kendras (Mercury and Jupiter for Gemini, Virgo, Sagittarius and Pisces lagnas) loses its natural beneficence and isn't treated as a benefic. `[1:04:18]`
- Such a planet does well when linked to a dusthana or a dusthana lord (Saturn with Mercury is good for Gemini lagna; Mars with Mercury is harmless). The rule is applied even though its reliability is unproven. `[1:05:19]`
- For this reason, its placement marks in a dusthana can be raised, up to about 70. `[1:04:18]`
- Mercury joined with the Sun counts as a malefic, and the kendradhipati dosha still applies to it. `[1:03:16]`
- Malefics score well in upachaya houses. `[1:04:18]`
- Viparita raja yoga is not assumed to deliver; a 12th lord in the 12th is left to observation. `[0:18:24]`
- Budhaditya yoga (Sun with Mercury) should form in a kendra. Elsewhere it still gives fine results, and the pair is good for Gemini lagna. `[1:22:41]`
- 6th lord in the 11th: good for service and jobs, gains, and victory over enemies (mainly one's own indiscipline and online foes). `[0:50:59]`
- A malefic 6th lord in the badhaka house is acceptable but brings overload; if retrograde, it can hurt health. `[0:26:31]`
- 6th and 8th lords in the 4th hurt the mother's (or the wife's) health, depending on the native's age when the dasha comes. `[1:42:02]`
- A 9th house linked to the 8th through its lord's star lord, especially with the Sun (father karaka) joined by Rahu, gives no inheritance and at most moral support; the native is self-made. `[0:30:35]`
- A 3rd house delivered from the 8th gives a complaining nature and, at times, harsh speech. `[1:14:31]`
- A 6th house delivered by an unafflicted planet (no node link) indicates good health. `[1:23:42]`
- Ketu in the 7th following a well-placed star lord (a yogakaraka in the 11th) doesn't deny marriage; expect only small breaks and quarrels over trivial things. `[1:23:42]`
- A house delivered through a Mars–Rahu combination becomes complicated and gives poor results. `[1:27:48]`
